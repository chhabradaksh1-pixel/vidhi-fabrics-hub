# Content strategy

Written 23 September 2026, from the site as it stands, `BRAND.md`, and an
inspection of what actually ranks for this business's terms.

The daily agent's Tuesday lens works against this file. `strategy.md` covers
technical SEO; this covers what to write and why.

## The competitive reality

Two searches decide the whole strategy.

**"wholesale fabric supplier Surat dress material"** returns IndiaMART,
Wholetex, Textile Zone, suratwholesaleshop, wholesalethankar, wholesalefactory
— marketplaces and directories, top to bottom. They win on thousands of SKUs,
published prices, COD and transactional signals. Vidhi Fabrics publishes no
prices and is not a marketplace, and `BRAND.md` forbids becoming one in the
copy. **This category is unwinnable and chasing it wastes everything.** A
supplier site will not out-inventory IndiaMART.

**"what GSM fabric for dress material shirting"** returns SewGuide, Silver
Bobbin, Senszio, Selvane, MFG Merch. Western consumer sewing blogs, every one.
No Indian result. No supplier. No B2B voice at all. They answer _"what weight
should my wardrobe be"_ — a home sewist's question.

Nobody is answering the trade buyer's version: **what do I write on the
purchase order, and what will the mill actually deliver against it?**

That is the gap, and it is unusually wide for a commercial niche. It exists
because the people who know the answer — mills, traders, suppliers — do not
write, and the people who write are not in the trade.

## Where this site can win

The searches worth owning are the ones a buyer makes **before** they know which
supplier to contact, where the answer requires trade knowledge rather than
inventory.

Three qualifying tests for any topic:

1. **Does a directory listing answer it?** If yes, skip it — they will outrank
   you forever.
2. **Would a Western sewing blog get it wrong?** If yes, that is the opening.
   Bulk sourcing is a different problem from home sewing and the existing
   content does not know that.
3. **Does the business actually know the answer from doing the work?** If it
   needs invention, it fails `BRAND.md` and it will read as thin.

The existing four articles all pass. This strategy extends the same logic
rather than changing direction.

## The clusters

### A. Specification literacy — the wedge

The highest-value cluster, because the SERP evidence shows nobody credible is
in it. Every piece answers a question a buyer asks before placing an order, and
answers it _from the supply side_.

- **What GSM actually means when you are ordering in bulk** — and why the
  number you specify and the number you receive can differ, what tolerance is
  normal, and how to write it on an order so there is no argument later. The
  Western blogs cannot write this; they have never had the argument.
- **Fabric width and what it does to your costing** — 44" against 58", and the
  arithmetic of why the cheaper metre is often the more expensive garment.
- **How to read a fabric spec** — GSM, width, composition, finish, and which of
  those are negotiable with a mill.
- **How much fabric a garment actually takes** — consumption by garment type,
  the thing every first-time bulk buyer gets wrong.
- **What "blend" means on a spec sheet** — composition percentages, what they
  do to drape, shrinkage and dye behaviour.

### B. Sourcing process — extend what works

Two articles already live here. The gap is what happens _between_ first contact
and bulk.

- **What to send a supplier when you do not know the fabric's name** — a photo,
  a swatch, a garment. The single most common buyer situation and nothing
  addresses it.
- **Strike-offs, lab dips and approvals** — what to approve, in what order, and
  what changes after you approve it.
- **What a fabric quality check actually covers** — and what it does not.

### C. Buyer-segment guides

`BRAND.md` names four audiences — retailers, brands, manufacturers, traders —
and no page speaks to any one of them. A boutique owner buying 50 metres and an
exporter buying 5,000 have almost nothing in common except the supplier.

One guide each. These also fix a positioning problem: `BRAND.md` explicitly
rejects copy that implies only large buyers are welcome, and a page written for
the boutique owner is the strongest possible proof that they are.

### D. Surat market literacy

The 1,784-word sourcing piece opened this and stopped.

- **Mill, trader, agent: who actually does what in Surat** — buyers routinely
  do not know which they are talking to, and it determines everything.
- **How fabric gets from Surat to your city** — transport, LR, e-way bill,
  packing, and realistic timing. Purely practical, nobody has written it, and
  it is the question that follows every quotation.
- **The Surat fabric calendar** — when the market is busy, when mills are
  committed, and what that means for when you should be placing an order. This
  one is evergreen and re-earns traffic every year.

### E. Fabric pages — the commercial layer

A page per catalogue fabric, targeting specification intent: "katan jacquard
GSM", "cotton linen blend for shirting", "denim canvas weight". Six pages, each
already backed by real spec data the business holds.

These are the closest thing to commercial intent the site can legitimately
compete for, and they connect the journal to the catalogue instead of leaving
the two unlinked.

## Sequence

Ordered by return, not by ease.

1. **The three GSM/width/spec pieces (A).** Widest gap, clearest authority,
   nothing comparable exists. Start with GSM — it is the term buyers actually
   type.
2. **The six fabric pages (E).** Most commercial, and the data exists.
3. **"What to send when you do not know the fabric's name" (B).** Highest
   real-world frequency of any question on this list.
4. **Transport from Surat (D).** Practical, unwritten, follows every quote.
5. **Buyer-segment guides (C).** Positioning value as much as search value.
6. **The Surat calendar (D).** Evergreen, seasonal compounding.

Depth beats count. One 1,800-word piece that genuinely answers a question
outperforms four 700-word pieces that gesture at one — which the existing four
already demonstrate, if traffic data ever becomes available to prove it.

## Constraints that shape every piece

From `BRAND.md`, and they are not negotiable:

- **No price, MOQ, lead-time or delivery claims.** This cuts off the obvious
  angle for half the topics above. Write the _method_ the buyer should use, not
  a promise about what this business offers.
- **Usable facts**: since the 1960s, 2,400+ active SKUs, 10+ export markets,
  12k m²/month, enquiry response typically within 12 hours. Nothing else is
  substantiated. The OEKO-TEX and GOTS claim may not be extended anywhere new
  until licence numbers exist.
- **Voice**: short declaratives, trade language, no superlatives, reader is a
  peer. A trader talking to a trader about cloth. Most SEO content fails this
  instantly by explaining what jacquard is.
- **Never imply enquiries get passed along.** One supplier answering for the
  order is the whole proposition.

## The Hindi question

This is the largest untapped opportunity on the site and it is blocked on
people, not on writing.

Hindi fabric-sourcing queries face a fraction of the English competition, the
buyer base genuinely searches in Hindi and Hinglish, and the site already has
the routing and hreflang built. Every article above is worth roughly double
because it can exist twice.

**But every Hindi and Hinglish string on this site was written by Claude and no
native speaker has ever reviewed any of it.** Publishing more unreviewed Hindi
compounds a known problem, and for a trade audience that reads register
instantly, bad Hindi damages credibility more than having no Hindi would.

The recommendation is not "write more Hindi". It is **get the existing Hindi
reviewed by a native speaker first** — `BRAND.md` already calls this the
highest-value outstanding brand task — and then treat Hindi as a first-class
content track rather than a translation step.

## Measured, 23 September 2026

A Search Console export now exists (`seo/reports/2026-09-23-search-console.md`
holds the full read). Three things in it change this document.

**The site is two weeks old in Search.** Twelve days of data, 9–20 September,
22 clicks and 130 impressions. The sequence above was written as though this
were an underperforming site needing optimisation. It is a new site in its
discovery phase, and the right posture is indexing hygiene and patience rather
than a campaign of tweaks.

**Hindi measurably outranks English on identical content.**
`/hi/blog/source-fabric-from-surat` sits at position 8.5; the English version of
the same article sits at 67. The Hindi hypothesis in this document was
reasoning; it is now evidence. Hindi moves from "someday" to the clearest
advantage the site has — and stays blocked on a native review of the existing
copy, which is no longer a nicety.

**Head commercial terms are confirmed unwinnable.** `dress material surat` at
14, `fabric wholesale in surat` at 21, `fabric manufacturers in surat` at 44.
Present, nowhere near competitive, exactly as the SERP inspection predicted.
The decision not to chase them stands.

The specification-literacy wedge remains **unproven either way**. No query in
the export touches it, because nothing has been written yet. The
best-performing article is a process-and-spec piece at position 10.5, which is
weak support and not evidence.

Re-export in a month. Twelve days at this volume is direction, not proof.
