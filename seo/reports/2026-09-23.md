### Gap 1 — no `BreadcrumbList` on any page

Articles live two levels down (`/` → `/blog` → `/blog/{slug}`) and the site
renders that hierarchy visually, but nothing declares it in markup. Google
therefore shows a bare URL in the result where it could show
`vidhifabrics.com › blog › sample-to-bulk-order-checklist`.

Low effort, applies to 8 pages, and it is one of the few rich results a
business site reliably gets. This is the strongest item today.

### Gap 2 — blog indexes describe themselves as a local business and nothing else

`/blog` and `/hi/blog` list four articles each and carry only the site-wide
`LocalBusiness` record. Nothing says "this is a blog, and here is what is on
it". A `Blog` or `ItemList` naming the four posts would let the index rank as a
hub rather than only as a page that happens to contain links.

Ties directly to warning **3** below: these are the two thinnest pages on the
site *and* the two with the least structured data.

### Considered and rejected — `Product` on the catalogue

The catalogue has six fabrics with SKU, GSM, width and composition, which looks
like an obvious `Product` candidate. I am not proposing it.

`Product` markup is only eligible for rich results when it carries `offers`
with a price and availability, and `BRAND.md` forbids stating prices, and the
business does not publish them. Product markup without offers gets ignored at
best and flagged as incomplete at worst. An `ItemList` of fabric names is
possible but buys almost nothing.

Revisit only if the business decides to publish indicative pricing.

## Proposed, in order

1. **Add `BreadcrumbList` to the 8 article pages and the 2 blog indexes.**
   Mechanical, no copy decisions, no claims involved. I can prepare this.
2. **Add `Blog`/`ItemList` to the two blog index pages.** Same, and it pairs
   with whatever is done about their thinness.
3. **Shorten the 5 long titles and the home description.** Needs care rather
   than effort: these are the strings a searcher reads before deciding to
   click, they are subject to `BRAND.md`'s claim rules, and per the no-churn
   rule they should then be left alone long enough to see an effect.
4. **Extend the blog index pages.** A sentence of framing per article would
   roughly double them and give the category page something to rank on.
