## One genuinely new finding

### Four images lost their intrinsic dimensions, five hours after the last report

`8e589c5` (22:08) and `5b4d56b` (22:14) on 26 September introduced the
illustrated logo and the full-bleed footer illustration. Both went in without
`width`/`height`. The 26 September report was written around 17:00, so it
could not have caught them — this is genuinely new, not a re-find.

- the logo `<img>`, in three places — `routes/index.tsx`, `routes/hi.tsx`,
  `components/fabric-desk.tsx` — so it affects every page on the site
- the footer illustration in `components/site-footer.tsx`

Every other `<img>` in the codebase (9 of them) has both attributes. This is a
consistency regression, not a pre-existing pattern.

**Honest caveat: I could not measure a benefit.** CLS on a full scroll-through
at 375×812 measured **0.000 both with and without the attributes** on a local
dev server. Images arrive too fast there for a shift to occur. The attributes
matter when images are slow relative to layout, which is the mid-range-Android
case this site's audience is in and which I cannot reproduce here.

So: correct, free, consistent with the rest of the codebase, and of unproven
value. It is in today's PR on that basis, not on a promised Core Web Vitals
win — and it is the _only_ code change in it.

## The consolidation finding: the data underneath the priority list has expired

The ranked list in `strategy.md` was written on 23 September from a Search
Console export covering **9–20 September**, when the site had twelve URLs.

Six of today's eighteen URLs — the three specification guides and their Hindi
twins — were published on 24 and 25 September. **They do not appear in the
export because they did not exist when it was taken.** Neither does the
deepened checklist article, whose improvement was priority #2 and which cannot

So a third of the site is invisible to the evidence the priority list is built
on, and the one item that was actioned cannot be scored. The 26 September
report asked for a fresh export too. Asking twice is the point: re-ranking
without one is reshuffling stale inputs.

### And one priority should come off the list entirely

23 September priority #3 was "diagnose why English `source-fabric-from-surat`
sits at 67 while its Hindi twin is at 8.5."

Re-reading the export: `/blog/source-fabric-from-surat` has **one impression**.
An average position over a single impression is one query, at one moment,
possibly irrelevant. There is no anomaly to diagnose — there is one data point.
The Hindi twin has two.

The same arithmetic weakens the headline Hindi finding more generally. It rests
on **five impressions across four Hindi URLs**. The 23 September report did say
"direction, not proof", but it then placed two priorities on top of it. At this
volume the GSC evidence for Hindi is close to none.

The _case_ for Hindi survives intact — it just does not come from Search
Console. It comes from the fact that nine of eighteen live URLs are in a
language no native speaker has ever reviewed, and that three more Hindi
articles were added this week. The review job grew by 60% while sitting at the
top of the priority list. That is a quality and credibility argument, and it
needs no impression data at all.

than mine. Summarised so this list is complete:

- **The loom video is 69% of the homepage.** 6,059,529 bytes,
  `autoplay loop muted playsinline`, no `poster`, no `preload`, rendering in a
  192 px band on a phone. Total first-visit weight 8.38 MB. Needs a re-encode,
  which needs ffmpeg, which is not available here. **Owner-side, and the single

  - That report also corrects `CLAUDE.md`'s note that it "revalidates on every
    visit" — it returns 304 with 0 bytes, so the 6 MB is a first-visit cost,
    not a per-visit one.

  needs on a retina display. A quality-versus-payload trade, left for the owner.

  fall back to `max-age=0, must-revalidate` and the `?v=` fingerprint on the
