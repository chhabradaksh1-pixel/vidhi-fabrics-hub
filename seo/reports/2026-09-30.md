### What the audit found: three articles had no publication date

`BlogPosting` emits `datePublished` conditionally — the route does
`...(article.published ? { datePublished: article.published } : {})`, which is
correct. The gap was in the data. Four of seven articles carried `published`;
three did not, so **six live URLs** (three articles × two languages) published
a `BlogPosting` with no date on it at all:

- `buying-dress-material-wholesale`
- `why-similar-sarees-have-different-prices`
- `sample-to-bulk-order-checklist`

This is an inconsistency inside one content type rather than a decision — the
three undated articles are simply the ones that predate the field being added.

**The date is in the repo, not invented.** The convention already in use is
exact: for all four dated articles, `published` equals the date of the commit
that introduced the article to `src/content/journal.ts`. Four for four.

| Article                     | Introducing commit | Declared `published` |
| --------------------------- | ------------------ | -------------------- |
| `source-fabric-from-surat`  | `0c91651` 2026-09-16 | 2026-09-16         |
| `fabric-width-and-costing`  | `285a9d2` 2026-09-24 | 2026-09-24         |
| `fabric-gsm-bulk-orders`    | `d9c537b` 2026-09-24 | 2026-09-24         |
| `how-to-read-a-fabric-spec` | `4641606` 2026-09-25 | 2026-09-25         |

All three undated articles entered in commit `2f1e275`, **2026-09-12**. Applying
the established rule gives them that date. That is reading the repo's own
record, not supplying a fact the business has not given.

Proposed and verified on the branch — see below.

## Found and deliberately not fixed

### `mainEntity: [Question]` on `BlogPosting` (10 URLs)

Five articles (× two languages) attach their FAQ to the `BlogPosting` as
`mainEntity`, an array of `Question`. On an `FAQPage` that is the correct
pattern. On a `BlogPosting` it says "the primary entity this article describes
is these questions", which is not what is meant.

It is **valid** schema.org — `mainEntity` ranges over `Thing` and `Question` is
a `Thing` — so it fails nothing, and it is not why I left it. I left it because
the payoff is not there: FAQ rich results were retired from Google Search on
7 May 2026 (`strategy.md`), so no Google surface reads this either way. The only
consumers that would benefit are Bing and the answer engines, and that benefit
is speculative. Against that, correcting it means restructuring markup on ten
live URLs.

If you want it corrected the right shape is an `@graph` carrying a `BlogPosting`
and a separate `FAQPage`, rather than nesting questions under the article. Say
the word and it is a small change — but it is not worth bundling with a fix that
should merge on its own evidence.

### `dateModified` — absent on all fourteen

Google lists it under recommended properties, and there is a concrete case for
it: the checklist article was deepened from 799 to 1,114 words on 26 September
and no structured data records that it changed.

Not proposed, because it needs a policy decision rather than a patch. Deriving
it from git's last-touch would bump the date on every typo fix, which is worse
than having no date. Maintaining it by hand means remembering to. **Owner's
call:** if you want it, the honest version is a `modified` field alongside
`published` in `journal.ts`, set only when an article is substantively revised.

### `LocalBusiness` — `priceRange` deliberately not proposed

Google lists `priceRange` under recommended properties for `LocalBusiness`, and
it is absent. **It should stay absent.** `BRAND.md` puts price claims off limits
— "Best price, lowest price, or direct-from-manufacturer pricing" — and the
rule covers structured data explicitly. There is no honest value to put there.
Recording the reasoning so a future pass does not "fix" it.

Also absent and lower stakes: `@id` (the block is emitted on all 18 pages as 18
unlinked copies rather than one referenced entity), `geo`, and `areaServed`.
None of these is worth a change on its own; they would be worth doing together
if the block is ever revisited.

### `/blog` and `/hi/blog` carry no `ItemList`

Both index pages list seven articles and emit no markup describing them. Worth
stating plainly: **Google's carousel rich result does not cover blog listings**
— it is limited to specific types — so adding `ItemList` would earn no rich
result. It would only help machine understanding of the listing. Low value,
noted rather than proposed.
